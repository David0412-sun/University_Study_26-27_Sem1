The proposed delivery system uses all seven parts of the Q1 framework: hardware and sensors, perception and data processing, positioning and SLAM, mission planning, motion planning, modelling and control, and GCS/HMI with communications (Chen, 2022, p. 21). The unmanned ground vehicle (UGV) provides a mobile ground station and recovery deck. I assume bounded vehicle speed and acceleration, a clear, approximately level deck, adequate payload capacity and battery reserve, and a balcony tray with clear overhead access and a registered visual identifier. These conditions define a practical setting for integrating the methods below.

Before departure, the GCS supplies the delivery manifest and operating constraints. A behaviour tree organises execution through conditions, actions and fallback branches (Colledanchise & Ögren, 2018). In this proposal, readiness checks cover propulsion, sensors, communication, payload retention and return energy. The tree advances when observations confirm completion, allowing failed searches or infeasible trajectories to trigger bounded retries or return. This connects mission decisions to feedback from the other modules throughout the flight.

Reliable state estimation is essential from the moving launch onward. An EKF-based visual-inertial estimator combines IMU predictions with camera corrections (Mourikis & Roumeliotis, 2007), while reliable GNSS updates and local SLAM mapping are added in this integration (Cadena et al., 2016). Calibrated observations of known deck-marker points provide relative pose through PnP (Lepetit, Moreno-Noguer, & Fua, 2009). Timestamped estimates are expressed in a common frame, allowing the UAV to maintain the deck's horizontal velocity while climbing clear and then transition into outbound flight.

During transit, I combine A* graph search (Hart, Nilsson, & Raphael, 1968) with constrained minimum-snap polynomial planning (Mellinger & Kumar, 2011; Richter, Bry, & Roy, 2016). The resulting timed references must satisfy obstacle clearance, speed and thrust limits. A rigid-body and rotor-thrust model supports cascaded P/PID control: position and velocity loops request thrust and attitude, while attitude and rate loops request moments. Velocity and acceleration feedforward assist tracking, and allocation produces four feasible rotor commands (PX4, n.d.). New obstacle observations require the planner to revise the trajectory.

Near the apartment building, a planned facade scan maintains visibility and clearance while camera and range measurements locate candidate balconies, people and obstacles. YOLO provides the detection method (Redmon, Divvala, Girshick, & Farhadi, 2016), with training adapted to balcony imagery. The system then checks the building, floor and apartment specified in the manifest against the registered identifier. This distinction matters because recognising a balcony does not establish the intended recipient. Uncertain identity or an obstructed approach prevents delivery.

Once identity and clearance are confirmed, the UAV hovers above the accessible tray. I propose a short winch that lowers the parcel while load or contact sensing checks that the tray supports it. Release is permitted only after this condition is met, and camera evidence confirms deposition before the mechanism retracts. These physical checks establish delivery beyond merely issuing a release command. The controller then updates its mass-dependent hover thrust to account for the removed payload.

The outcome must also reach the truck. MAVLink carries timestamped vehicle-motion estimates, together with battery and system-status telemetry (MAVLink, n.d.). The proposed application sends a delivery record containing the target identity, outcome, time and evidence, with acknowledgement and retries to verify reception. Report reception and physical delivery remain separate completion conditions; a protocol command acknowledgement alone establishes neither. High-rate stabilisation stays onboard, so flight control does not depend on each telemetry exchange.

Recovery closes the mission by updating the moving rendezvous and tracking deck-relative position and velocity (Borowczyk et al., 2017). My design permits descent only with alignment, low horizontal relative speed and reliable tracking. During descent, it continues to match the deck's horizontal motion, reducing vertical relative speed towards zero as it reaches touchdown. Stale updates, marker loss or excessive deck motion require a go-around or safe alternative within the energy reserve. These gates and tolerances require platform-specific validation; returning to the original launch coordinates would miss the moving UGV.
